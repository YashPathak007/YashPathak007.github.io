02 — THE METHOD

Make the state legible.
Make the boundary explicit.

The same product discipline runs through every repository. Choose a node to see the decision behind the interface.

TRUSTis a product feature

01 / START WITH THE WORK

Translate a vague concern into a real next action.

Good security UX starts before the alert, upload, or share link. I model who is acting, what they are trying to protect, and which decision the system needs to support.

  • 01Name the operator and the risk.
  • 02Reduce the first decision to a clear surface.
  • 03Keep the workflow small enough to verify.

02 / SHOW THE SYSTEM

Every important state gets a visible shape.

Queued, empty, invalid, expired, offline, failed, and completed are product states—not implementation details. The interface should tell a person where they are without requiring a console.

  • 01Make progress and ownership obvious.
  • 02Design the recovery path with the happy path.
  • 03Use language that survives a stressful moment.

03 / LEAVE A TRAIL

Connect a result to something another person can inspect.

Repositories, audit trails, evidence reports, and repeatable checks make a claim useful. The work should remain understandable after the demo ends.

  • 01Document the path from setup to result.
  • 02Separate observed evidence from interpretation.
  • 03Keep the proof close to the decision.

04 / SAY WHERE IT STOPS

Honesty is a feature that protects the whole system.

Security-minded work is not a promise of certainty. I make scope, assumptions, threat boundaries, research baselines, and failure modes visible so the product earns trust without performing it.

  • 01State the protection and the limitation.
  • 02Prefer a bounded claim to a dramatic one.
  • 03Give the next human decision enough context.
